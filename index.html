Essex County, New Jersey

Volunteer radio communicators supporting Essex County’s responders and community partners

Essex County Amateur Radio Emergency Service (ARES) is a local group of licensed Amateur Radio operators who volunteer their communications skills in support of government and non-governmental partners before, during, and after emergencies. We train year-round and can establish reliable voice and data radio links when regular systems are overloaded, disrupted, or otherwise unavailable. We also provide reliable communications and an extra layer of safety at community events.

What we can provide

  • Alternate communications paths using Amateur Radio, including portable and simplex capabilities that can operate independently of cellular and Internet infrastructure
  • Staffing for EOCs, shelters, hospitals, field sites, and community events
  • Communications for 5K/10K/half/marathon runs, cycling events, large community gatherings
  • County-wide communication nets and directed traffic handling
  • Field-expedient antennas and portable stations
  • Voice and message traffic, including partner-preferred forms (e.g., ICS-213) via radio or radio-email

ARES augments—not replaces—public-safety communications and integrates with the requesting organization’s incident structure and operating procedures.

Who we are

About ARES

Prepared volunteers, established procedures, and radio systems that can operate independently of conventional communications infrastructure.

Mission & partners

ARES is a program of ARRL, The National Association for Amateur Radio®. Our volunteers support government agencies (local, county, state, tribal, federal) and non-governmental organizations such as relief/humanitarian groups. We prepare in advance through planning, training, exercises, and regular nets so we’re ready when asked to assist.

How we fit into emergency management

ARES works within the Incident Command System (ICS) when used by the requesting organization and follows that organization’s procedures. When assigned within an ICS structure, ARES personnel work through the incident’s established chain of command, while ARES leadership coordinates personnel, capabilities, and program responsibilities with the requesting organization. We can interface with the National Traffic System (NTS) and use radio-email, such as Winlink, when appropriate to deliver and track partner messages.

Flexible communications support for fixed facilities, field operations, planned events, and outages.

Multi-band voice and data using VHF/UHF/HF; repeaters and accepted simplex channels; portable and fixed stations; point-to-point and net operations.

When support is needed

Activation & requests

ARES integrates with the requesting organization’s incident structure and operating procedures.

How to request ARES support
Agencies may request ARES through the Essex County Emergency Coordinator. Please include: point of contact, incident/event description, locations, timeframe, safety considerations, and any preferred forms (e.g., ICS-213) or operational constraints. ARES will coordinate with your incident structure and provide an ARES point of contact to integrate with your team.

Go to contact information

What to expect during activation
  • Assignment of an ARES point of contact and, when requested, appropriately qualified personnel to support the incident Communications Unit.
  • Integration with your ICS organization and compliance with your policies.
  • Personnel prepared for self-sufficient field deployment when the assignment requires it.
  • Message handling using partner-preferred forms and radio-email where appropriate.
  • Participation in after-action reviews to drive continuous improvement.
Get involved

Volunteer & training

Licensed Amateur Radio operators can participate, build experience, and advance through additional training and qualification levels.

Who can join

Any FCC-licensed Amateur Radio operator with an interest in public service is eligible to participate in ARES. ARRL membership is not required to join; training and qualification requirements apply for higher participation levels and certain leadership roles.

Training path (high-level)

  • Basic – Local ARES orientation; ARRL ARES Basic EmComm; FEMA IS-100, IS-200, IS-700 and IS-800; ARES Position Task Book (Level 1).
  • Intermediate – ARRL Basic & Intermediate EmComm; FEMA IS-100/200/700/800; ARES PTB (Level 2). ARRL membership required for this level.
  • Advanced – ARRL Advanced EmComm; FEMA PDS (IS-230/240/241/242/244), IS-288; ARES PTB (Level 3); ICS-300/400 encouraged where available locally. ARRL membership required.
  • Specialized – Additional preparation for ARESMAT or extended deployments; CISA AUXCOMM training encouraged where available.

Leadership training: Designated ARES leadership positions have additional ARRL training requirements, including the ARES Leadership course.

ARES also cooperates with CERT/VOAD and can coordinate with NTS for traffic handling; groups may use radio-email (e.g., Winlink) when appropriate.
